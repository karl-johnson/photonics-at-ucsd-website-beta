We have purposely included many demos on this site that don’t require traditional optical lab equipment, but many (particularly more complex ones) do. A well-equipped optics lab almost always contains thousands of dollars of optomechanics: the breadboards, posts, mounts, and many other helpful components that allow optics to be rigidly mounted and fine-tuned into perfect alignment. However, the high cost makes many of these components inaccessible to all but the most well-funded groups and organizations. On this page we discuss which optomechanics have affordable DIY alternatives and which we think are worth buying.

## Summary

| Item | Off-the-shelf cost (per optic) | DIY cost / required tools | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Optical breadboards | [$200–$1000](https://www.thorlabs.com/solid-aluminum-optical-breadboards) | $10–$200; quality improves with more tools, supplies and effort | Off-the-shelf options are so expensive that DIY is often worth it, but off-the-shelf is *super* nice |
| Fixed lens holders | [$15–$30](https://www.thorlabs.com/fixed-lens-mounts) | <$1 with a 3D printer | DIY is a bit bulkier and must use a clamp instead of a retaining ring design, but is functionally almost identical |
| Irises | [$60–75](https://www.thorlabs.com/post-mountable-iris-diaphragms) | <$1 with a 3D printer | Simple 3D printed holes can perform the same function for many alignment purposes, but the adjustable size of irises is sometimes useful |
| Viewing screens | [$30](https://www.thorlabs.com/post-mountable-viewing-screens) | $0–5; a 3D printer allows for really nice solutions | Do not buy this. The plastic Thorlabs screens we have used have been WORSE than a cheap DIY solution |
| Kinematic tip/tilt mounts | [$45](https://www.thorlabs.com/kinematic-mirror-mounts-with-two-adjusters) | *Untested* | Thorlabs is not too expensive for what you get, so we’ve always bought these |
| Posts | [$5](https://www.thorlabs.com/optical-posts-half-inch-and-12-mm) | *Untested* | Hard to DIY out of metal, and doesn’t work well as plastic |
| Post holders | [$10–15](https://www.thorlabs.com/half-inch-post-holders) | *Untested:* <$1 with a 3D printer | DIY holder is aight; the $3 [knob](https://www.thorlabs.com/item/TS25H-P5) is absolutely worth buying if you DIY |
| Bases | [$5–10](https://www.thorlabs.com/slotted-bases) | *Untested:* <$1 with a 3D printer | A 3D printed solution seems like it’d be just as good as off-the-shelf aluminum |
| Base clamps | $5–10: [old style](https://www.thorlabs.com/table-clamps) vs [pedestal fork style](https://www.thorlabs.com/clamping-forks-for-pedestal-posts) | *Untested:* <$1 with a 3D printer | You will likely sacrifice a lot of security/ability to really tighten things down if this is plastic, but DIY could work if necessary |
